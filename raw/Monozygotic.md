Monozygotic is a biological term for identical twins, meaning the individuals developed from a single fertilized egg (zygote) that split into two. Because they originate from the exact same egg and sperm, monozygotic twins share nearly 100% of their DNA and are always the same sex.

The scientific and social details regarding monozygotic twins include:

- How They Form: A single egg is fertilized by a single sperm to form one zygote. Shortly after fertilization, this cell mass divides into two separate embryos that develop independently.
- Genetics vs. Fraternal: Unlike fraternal (dizygotic) twins—which result from two separate eggs and share only half their genes like standard siblings—monozygotic twins share essentially identical genomes.
- Rate of Occurrence: Natural monozygotic twinning is an embryological event that occurs randomly in roughly 3 to 4 out of every 1,000 births worldwide.
- Physical Uniqueness: While they are genetically identical, subtle differences can still exist. Epigenetic changes (how genes are expressed over time) and differing environments in the womb can result in unique fingerprints or minor physical disparities.
- Medical Research: Because their genetic code is fundamentally the same, monozygotic twins are incredibly valuable in scientific research for distinguishing between the effects of genetics (nature) and environment (nurture).
