# Dark Energy

## Definition

Dark energy is a hypothetical form of energy permeating all of space that is thought to be responsible for the observed accelerating expansion of the Universe. In the Lambda-CDM model it is represented by the cosmological constant Λ (Lambda) and constitutes approximately 68% of the total mass-energy content of the Universe.

## Key Characteristics

- Acts as a repulsive force on cosmological scales, counteracting gravity and driving the Universe's expansion to accelerate.
- Represented by Λ (the cosmological constant) in the Lambda-CDM model — effectively a placeholder for an as-yet poorly understood physical phenomenon.
- Constitutes roughly 68% of the total mass-energy budget of the Universe, making it the dominant component.
- Its fundamental nature remains unknown; physicists lack a confirmed microscopic or field-theoretic description of what dark energy actually is.

## Applications

- Central to models of the late-time evolution and ultimate fate of the Universe.
- Studied via observations of Type Ia supernovae, the CMB, and baryon acoustic oscillations to constrain its equation of state.
- Motivates extensions to general relativity and candidate theories such as [quintessence](../concepts/quintessence.md) or modified gravity.
