# WIMPs

## Definition

WIMPs (Weakly Interacting Massive Particles) are a class of hypothetical elementary particles that are among the leading candidates for the identity of cold dark matter. They are predicted to interact with ordinary matter only through gravity and the weak nuclear force, making them electrically neutral, stable over cosmological timescales, and effectively invisible to telescopes — consistent with the observed properties of dark matter.

## Key Characteristics

- Masses typically in the range of ~10 GeV to ~1 TeV (roughly 10 to 1000 times the mass of a proton), though exact predictions depend on the specific WIMP model.
- Interact via the weak nuclear force and gravity only — no electromagnetic or strong nuclear interactions, so they do not emit or absorb light.
- Naturally arise in several well-motivated extensions to the Standard Model of particle physics, most notably supersymmetry (SUSY), where the lightest supersymmetric particle (LSP) is often a WIMP candidate.
- Their predicted relic abundance from the early Universe matches the observed dark matter density — this coincidence is known as the "WIMP miracle" and has historically motivated WIMP models.
- Despite extensive experimental searches (direct detection, indirect detection, and collider production), no confirmed WIMP signal has been found as of the mid-2020s, placing strong constraints on many WIMP models.

## Applications

- Target of direct detection experiments (e.g., LUX-ZEPLIN, XENONnT) that search for rare WIMP-nucleus scattering events in ultra-low-background detectors.
- Target of indirect detection searches looking for annihilation products (gamma rays, neutrinos, positrons) from regions of high dark matter density.
- Searched for at the Large Hadron Collider via missing transverse energy signatures.
