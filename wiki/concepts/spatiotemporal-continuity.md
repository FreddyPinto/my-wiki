# Spatiotemporal Continuity

Spatiotemporal continuity is a philosophical criterion for the identity of objects over time. Under this view, an object retains its identity as long as it maintains an unbroken chain of existence through space and time — regardless of how much its material composition changes.

## Core Claim

An object is the same object at two different times if and only if there is a continuous spatiotemporal path connecting its earlier and later stages. Physical replacement of parts does not break identity, so long as the object is never "gapped" — never ceases to exist and is later reconstituted from scratch.

## Application to the Ship of Theseus

In the context of the [Ship of Theseus / Identity Paradox](../concepts/ship-of-theseus-identity-paradox.md):

- **Ship A** (the continuously maintained ship): retains identity under spatiotemporal continuity because it has existed without interruption.
- **Ship B** (rebuilt from the original planks): does *not* retain identity under this criterion; it is a new object reconstructed from old materials, not a continuous extension of the original.

## Contrast with Perdurantism

[Perdurantism](../concepts/perdurantism.md) also appeals to temporal extension but treats objects as four-dimensional wholes composed of temporal parts, rather than as three-dimensional objects that persist through time by having the same identity at each moment.
