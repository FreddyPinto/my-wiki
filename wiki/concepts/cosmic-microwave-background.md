# Cosmic Microwave Background

## Definition

The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) is the thermal radiation left over from the early Universe, approximately 380,000 years after the Big Bang, when the Universe cooled enough for protons and electrons to combine into neutral hydrogen and the Universe became transparent to light. It is observed today as a nearly uniform glow of microwave radiation permeating the entire sky.

## Key Characteristics

- Represents the oldest observable electromagnetic signal in the Universe, providing a snapshot of conditions roughly 380,000 years after the Big Bang.
- Has a near-perfect blackbody spectrum with a temperature of approximately 2.725 K.
- Tiny anisotropies (temperature fluctuations of ~1 part in 100,000) in the CMB encode information about the density, composition, and geometry of the early Universe.
- One of the primary observational predictions of the Lambda-CDM model; ΛCDM accurately reproduces the observed CMB power spectrum.
- Measurements of the CMB yield one of the two key inputs to the Hubble Tension: the early-universe expansion rate estimate.

## Applications

- Used to constrain cosmological parameters including the Hubble constant, matter density, dark energy density, and the geometry of the Universe.
- Basis for major space missions: COBE, WMAP, and Planck.
- Provides indirect evidence for the existence of cold dark matter through its effects on the CMB power spectrum.
