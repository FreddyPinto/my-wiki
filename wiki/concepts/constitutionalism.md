# Constitutionalism

Constitutionalism (in metaphysics) is a position on object identity that holds an object is defined by both its current material composition and its history. It stands in contrast to views that privilege either matter alone or spatiotemporal continuity alone.

## Core Claim

An object's identity is constituted by the union of what it is made of *now* and the historical chain of events that brought it to its current form. Neither matter nor history is alone sufficient — both are necessary components of what makes something the thing it is.

## Application to the Ship of Theseus

In the context of the [Ship of Theseus / Identity Paradox](../concepts/ship-of-theseus-identity-paradox.md), constitutionalism yields an inclusive verdict:

- **Ship A** (the continuously maintained ship) has a valid claim to being the real Ship of Theseus on the basis of its unbroken history and continuous existence.
- **Ship B** (rebuilt from the original planks) also has a valid — but distinct — claim, grounded in its material continuity with the original object.

Rather than declaring one ship the winner, constitutionalism holds that both ships are, in different senses, the Ship of Theseus. The paradox is genuine rather than resolvable by picking a single criterion.

## Contrast with Other Positions

| Position | Identity Criterion | Verdict on Ship A vs Ship B |
|---|---|---|
| [Spatiotemporal Continuity](../concepts/spatiotemporal-continuity.md) | Unbroken existence in space and time | Ship A wins; Ship B is a replica |
| Constitutionalism | Current matter + history | Both ships have valid claims |
| [Perdurantism](../concepts/perdurantism.md) | Four-dimensional whole of all temporal parts | Both ships are overlapping temporal segments of one four-dimensional object |
