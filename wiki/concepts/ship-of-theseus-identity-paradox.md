# Ship of Theseus / Identity Paradox

The Ship of Theseus is a classical thought experiment that probes the nature of identity and persistence through change. It asks: if an object has every one of its components replaced over time, is it still the same object?

## The Paradox

The thought experiment has two stages:

1. **Gradual replacement**: Theseus's ship has its planks replaced one by one as they decay until no original plank remains. Is it still the same ship?
2. **Hobbes's twist** (added by [Thomas Hobbes](../entities/thomas-hobbes.md)): If someone collects all the discarded original planks and rebuilds a second ship from them, two ships now exist — Ship A (continuously maintained) and Ship B (reconstructed from originals). Which is the *real* Ship of Theseus?

## Philosophical Positions

### Spatiotemporal Continuity
Ship A is the real ship. It has maintained an unbroken chain of existence in time and space; Ship B is a reconstruction — a replica of the past, not the original.
See: [Spatiotemporal Continuity](../concepts/spatiotemporal-continuity.md)

### Constitutionalism
An object's identity is defined by both its current matter and its history. Both ships have valid — but different — claims to being the real ship.

### Perdurantism
An object is not merely a three-dimensional thing at a single moment but a four-dimensional entity that extends through time. "The Ship of Theseus" is the entire collection of all its temporal stages from origin to present.
See: [Perdurantism](../concepts/perdurantism.md)

## Applications

- **Personal identity**: Human cells are continuously replaced; we are biologically a different collection of atoms than we were at birth, yet we consider ourselves the same person. See: [Personal Identity](../concepts/personal-identity.md)
- **Organizations and brands**: Companies replace employees, leadership, and processes over time. At what point does an institution lose its original identity?

## Origin

Documented by [Plutarch](../entities/plutarch.md), who described the Athenians preserving [Theseus](../entities/theseus.md)'s ship in [Athens](../entities/athens.md).
