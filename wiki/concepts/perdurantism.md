# Perdurantism

Perdurantism (also called four-dimensionalism) is a metaphysical position on the persistence of objects through time. It holds that objects should not be understood as three-dimensional things that exist wholly at each moment, but as four-dimensional entities that extend through time just as they extend through space.

## Core Claim

Under perdurantism, an object is the *mereological sum* of all its temporal parts — i.e., the complete collection of all its stages from its beginning to its end. Asking "is it the same object?" at two different times is analogous to asking whether two spatial slices belong to the same four-dimensional whole.

## Application to the Ship of Theseus

In the context of the [Ship of Theseus / Identity Paradox](../concepts/ship-of-theseus-identity-paradox.md), the "Ship of Theseus" under perdurantism is the entire four-dimensional object stretching from its original construction to its final state. Both Ship A and Ship B are partial overlapping regions of that four-dimensional whole, which dissolves the paradox rather than choosing a winner.

## Contrast with Spatiotemporal Continuity (Endurantism)

[Spatiotemporal continuity](../concepts/spatiotemporal-continuity.md) (a form of endurantism) treats objects as three-dimensional things that persist *wholly* through time — the same object is present at each moment. Perdurantism rejects this, saying no single temporal slice is the complete object.
