# Hubble Tension

## Definition

The Hubble Tension is an unresolved discrepancy in cosmology between two independently derived measurements of the Hubble constant (H₀) — the rate at which the Universe is currently expanding. Measurements derived from the early Universe (via the Cosmic Microwave Background) consistently yield a lower value than measurements derived from the local Universe (via Type Ia supernovae and the cosmic distance ladder), and the gap is statistically significant enough that it may indicate new physics beyond the Lambda-CDM model.

## Key Characteristics

- The discrepancy is between early-universe measurements (~67–68 km/s/Mpc, from CMB data) and local-universe measurements (~73–74 km/s/Mpc, from supernovae and Cepheid distance ladders).
- The tension has grown in statistical significance over time as measurement precision has improved, making systematic error alone an increasingly unlikely explanation.
- Represents one of the most prominent anomalies threatening the completeness of the Lambda-CDM model.
- Potential explanations include unidentified systematic errors, early dark energy, additional relativistic species, or modifications to the standard cosmological model.

## Applications

- Actively researched as a potential signal of physics beyond ΛCDM.
- Motivates new observational campaigns (e.g., JWST, Euclid, DESI) designed to independently cross-check the Hubble constant.
- Drives theoretical work exploring extensions to ΛCDM and alternative cosmological models.
