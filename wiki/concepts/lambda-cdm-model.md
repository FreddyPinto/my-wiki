# Lambda-CDM Model

## Definition

The Lambda-CDM (ΛCDM) model is the standard mathematical framework of modern cosmology. It describes how the Universe evolves from the Big Bang to its present state, accounting for cosmic expansion, the formation of large-scale structure, and the composition of the Universe's total mass-energy budget.

## Key Characteristics

- Named for its two primary unknown components: Λ (Lambda, representing dark energy) and CDM (Cold Dark Matter).
- Estimates the Universe's mass-energy budget as approximately 5% normal (baryonic) matter, 27% cold dark matter, and 68% dark energy.
- Uses only a small number of free adjustable parameters yet achieves broad predictive accuracy.
- Predicts the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) radiation pattern, the large-scale web-like distribution of galaxies, and the primordial abundance of light elements.
- The model posits that the Universe began with the Big Bang and has been expanding ever since, with the expansion currently accelerating due to dark energy.

## Applications

- Serves as the baseline framework against which all cosmological observations are compared and interpreted.
- Used to model galaxy formation and the large-scale structure of the universe.
- Provides the theoretical foundation for missions measuring the CMB (e.g., WMAP, Planck) and large-scale galaxy surveys.
- Informs estimates of the age and geometry of the Universe.
