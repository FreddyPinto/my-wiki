# Lambda-CDM

[Lambda-CDM.md](../../raw/Lambda-CDM.md)

## Summary

This source describes the Lambda-CDM (ΛCDM) model, the standard mathematical framework of modern cosmology. It covers the model's two primary components — dark energy (Λ) and cold dark matter (CDM) — as well as its core principles, predictive power, and known limitations including the Hubble Tension and uncertainties about the nature of the dark sector.

## Key Points

- ΛCDM describes the evolution of the Universe from the Big Bang to the present, explaining cosmic expansion, galaxy formation, and the large-scale structure of the universe.
- The model estimates the mass-energy budget of the Universe as approximately 5% normal matter, 27% dark matter, and 68% dark energy.
- ΛCDM accurately predicts the Cosmic Microwave Background radiation, the web-like large-scale distribution of galaxies, and the abundance of light elements.
- The "Hubble Tension" is an unresolved discrepancy between expansion-rate measurements derived from the early universe (CMB) versus the local universe (supernovae).
- Dark energy and dark matter remain poorly understood at a fundamental level; Λ and CDM are effectively placeholders for unknown physics.
- On small scales, ΛCDM predictions sometimes clash with observed dwarf galaxy densities and kinematics.
