# Monozygotic

[Monozygotic.md](../../raw/Monozygotic.md)

## Summary

Monozygotic (identical) twins develop from a single fertilized egg that splits into two embryos shortly after fertilization. Because they originate from the same egg and sperm, monozygotic twins share nearly 100% of their DNA and are always the same sex. Despite this genetic identity, epigenetic changes and differing intrauterine environments can produce subtle physical differences between them, and they are widely used in nature-versus-nurture research for precisely this reason.

## Key Points

- A single zygote splits to produce two genetically identical embryos; natural occurrence rate is ~3–4 per 1,000 births worldwide.
- Monozygotic twins are distinct from dizygotic (fraternal) twins, which result from two separately fertilized eggs and share only ~50% of their genes.
- Genetic identity does not guarantee physical identity: epigenetic changes and womb environment differences produce unique fingerprints and minor physical disparities.
- Because genetic variables are held constant, monozygotic twins are the primary tool for isolating environmental (nurture) effects from genetic (nature) effects in scientific research.
- The source connects to personal identity: two individuals can be genetically identical yet become distinct persons, implying that identity is constituted by more than genetics alone.
